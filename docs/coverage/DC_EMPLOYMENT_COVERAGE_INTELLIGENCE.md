# DC Employment Coverage Intelligence — Pilot Report

**Issue [#19](https://github.com/murphym123/career-discovery-engine/issues/19) · Research date: November 6, 2026 · Research only**

## Executive answer

The engine has demonstrably incomplete DC coverage, including gaps in accessible policy and civic-sector hiring inventories. **The percentage of the entire market covered is not identifiable from the available evidence.** Neither the earlier informal *0.1–0.3% of all jobs* nor *1–5% of relevant jobs* survives as an evidence-based estimate. They should be withdrawn as estimates, rather than adjusted to another unsupported range.

The latest actual [manual run #42193251891](https://github.com/murphym123/career-discovery-engine/actions/runs/42193251891) retained **3,288 sightings**, but only **216 regional-labelled listing identities**, of which **132 had descriptions**. Those are not 216 confirmed current, paid, distinct vacancies. This independent, explicitly nonrepresentative 32-endpoint audit found **43 additional regional-labelled listings across six accessible inventories**, with **no exact identity or normalized employer/title match in that captured run**. Four inspected examples plausibly accommodate 0–2 years, with material qualifications still to check; several other attractive titles do not. These are real retrieved records, not test vacancies or newly implemented engine gains.

The strongest next investments are: **(1) discover and poll publication endpoints using actual regional evidence rather than URL hints; (2) support public iCIMS and embedded Paylocity inventories; (3) correct geographic scope and structured location aliases; (4) bring lawful public-sector and institutional channels into the DC collection; (5) measure inventory and description completeness explicitly.** Ranking changes would not repair these upstream gaps.

## What is being counted

| Denominator | Operational definition | What is measurable now? |
|---|---|---|
| A. All current paid vacancies | Distinct externally advertised paid positions accepting candidates at a common date, with a worksite offered in the full Washington MSA. Exclude closed ads, resume pools, events, volunteering and duplicated requisitions. Multiple openings behind one ad remain an unknown. | No compatible enumerated population. No market-wide point estimate, interval or lower/upper coverage bound. |
| B. Broad early-career professional opportunities | Subset of A spanning policy, government affairs, lobbying, research, consulting, advocacy, communications, programs, development, administration, operations, executive support and credible adjacent functions; typically compatible with 0–2 years, allowing explicit preferences and degree/eligibility constraints to be reviewed. | No independent occupation/qualification census. Job titles and vendor “entry-level” labels cannot supply the denominator. |
| C. Known accessible publisher inventories | Listing identities actually exposed in dated lawful public inventories, with declared totals or explicitly bounded visible windows. Apply A/B only after pay, currentness and qualifications are checked. | Source-local metadata enumeration and overlap can be measured. A complete paid-vacancy denominator is still unknown, even for the six inventories. |

A **sighting** is a source observation; a **listing** is a published record; a **vacancy** is a hiring need. An endpoint is not an employer; a staffing firm or job board can publish many employers' positions. An employer name group is not a verified legal entity. One job with several offered locations is counted once here, not once per city.

Strict DC means the District itself. “DMV” is ambiguous and is not a reproducible market boundary. For the broader benchmark comparison, the Washington MSA uses DC; Maryland's Charles, Frederick, Montgomery and Prince George's counties; Virginia's Arlington, Clarke, Culpeper, Fairfax, Fauquier, Loudoun, Prince William, Rappahannock, Spotsylvania, Stafford and Warren counties, plus Alexandria, Fairfax, Falls Church, Fredericksburg, Manassas and Manassas Park cities; and Jefferson County, WV. Baltimore is outside this boundary. This is the [23-jurisdiction BLS delineation](https://www.bls.gov/regions/mid-atlantic/news-release/2026/occupationalemploymentandwages_washingtondc_20260710.htm), not an assertion that every location is a practical commute. The engine currently describes its scope as DC plus an existing nearby-MD/VA fixture, which is narrower and not interchangeable with that MSA.

Eligible US remote opportunities are a separate supplemental population. A DC employer address, “Headquarters,” country-only label, or a title mentioning remote does not alone establish a DC physical worksite or permission to work from DC.

### Failure origins are different

- **Retrieval/access:** Senate's regional route returned 404, then the advertised home returned 403. This is failure, not a successful zero. WFN previously failed during robots retrieval but now produced 21 records; that history does not establish a permanent ban. No denial was bypassed.
- **Known source not polled:** Public Citizen's supported JazzHR board is already in the saved registry, with `last_checked_run: null`. Its sitemap hints label “Executive Assistant” and other URL-only titles `elsewhere`; there is no actual worksite in those URL hints. Existing selection therefore lacks reliable regional evidence for a real DC board.
- **Undiscovered supported inventory:** ACLU and FGS Global's exact Greenhouse board keys are absent from the saved registry, despite accessible documented public inventories. This is different from needing a new adapter.
- **Missing inventory extraction:** Public iCIMS pages expose real server-rendered cards. Paylocity's visible JavaScript warning coexists with a public embedded `Jobs` array containing two records and structured addresses. A shell or warning must not be interpreted as a successful empty search.
- **Geographic scope/classification:** A limited locality review finds **40 more retrieved identities in MSA localities** labelled `elsewhere`, across six employer-name groups. None has a description in this capture. The 256-identity diagnostic union is an expanded-location metadata set, not 256 verified vacancies. It includes specialist, retail/clinical and recruiting-event material, so adding 40 to a “suitable jobs” count would be wrong.

The 40 include Fort/Ft. Belvoir, Chantilly, Quantico, Frederick MD, Stafford VA, Fredericksburg, Lanham, Landover and Beltsville. Locality membership is supported by [OPM's Fairfax locality list](https://www.opm.gov/services-for-agencies/administrative-law-judges/counties-cities/fairfax-county-va/), [Virginia's Quantico charter](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/charters/quantico/), [Maryland's Frederick entry](https://www.msa.maryland.gov/msa/mdmanual/37mun/frederick/html/f.html), the MSA county/city delineation above, and [Prince George's County's community service areas](https://www.princegeorgescountymd.gov/departments-offices/fire-emergency-medical-services/office-fire-chief/our-stations). This manual mapping is deliberately limited, not a full geographic parser. Stafford NJ, Baltimore-area localities and an unresolved “Herdon” typo are not silently reassigned. Twenty-four of the 40 come from Maania, seven Direct Demo, five The Joint Chiropractic, two Visiting Angels, and one each USDA and SAIC. Visiting Angels includes recruiting-event material. The location labels themselves are not independently verified worksites.

Separately, the engine's current classifier returns `elsewhere` for **`US-DC` and `US-DC-Washington`**, the actual aliases on AEI's and Brookings' inventories. Even discovering those boards would leave a downstream geographic false-negative risk without alias handling. The [frozen classifier cases](data/geography-cases.json) record this direct DC alias problem separately from the broader-MSA scope decision.

## Independent endpoint audit

The [32-endpoint frame](data/sample-frame.json) was fixed at **09:58:06 UTC**, before checking exact membership in the latest registry/ledger. Four examples were selected in each of eight strata using external institutional/channel knowledge and the saved search prompts. [Freeze receipts](data/freeze-receipts.json) preserve the hashes of the original frozen objects.

This is **purposeful, nonprobability sampling**, weighted toward visible institutions and channels. It underrepresents small/local firms, less visible employers, additional universities, local governments, contractors and non-English/offline hiring. Three known access controls—Senate, House official channels and ASAE—are explicitly separated from 29 new checks. The [saved historical access observations](../issue17/coverage-excerpt.json) substantiate the House/ASAE controls; they were not rechecked here. It is independent *source evidence*, not a blinded independent analyst: the AI researcher has implementation context. No selection probabilities, survey weights or confidence intervals exist. No engine code was tuned against this sample, and it should be treated as development evidence for future work, not a reusable independent validation set.

Public reads respected robots, delays and access denials. No accounts, private/undocumented inventory calls, hidden Workday CXS requests, challenge interaction or paid API were used. Some redirects or advertised portals were left unexhausted under the bounded audit. These are unknowns, not access prohibitions. Robots-unavailable means permission could not be established in this environment, not that the publisher universally bans retrieval. Primary web research supplements HTTP receipts where explicitly noted.

## Independent benchmarks and the proposed market estimate

| Evidence | What it counts | Why it cannot be a coverage denominator here |
|---|---|---|
| [Aspen Tech Labs Q3 report](https://aspentechlabs.com/jobmarketpulse-reports/2026/jobmarketpulse-report-q3-2026): **165,437** metro postings | Monthly unique vacancies active at any point in the reference month; September reference is inferred from the report's Q3 comparison method. Employer-site panel; agencies excluded from Q2 unless stated otherwise. | Monthly union is not a November 6 stock; panel and geography matching are not independently reconciled. |
| [Headcount Labs Oct 14 snapshot](https://headcountlabs.com/reports/us-job-market/2026-10-14/): **50,772** metro jobs | Provider-observed open employer/ATS postings; snapshot `mi-20261014T234333Z`; copies merged and multi-metro jobs counted in each metro. | Dated 23 days earlier; provider population and exact identity overlap with engine unknown. |
| [Second Summit Nov 2 metro page](https://secondsummit.ai/jobs-data/metro/washington-arlington-alexandria-dc-va-md-wv/): **5,687** postings / **857** employers in its data | A provider's observed metro subset. These figures were verified in a primary-domain search excerpt; the full page/method was unavailable. Lower confidence than a retrieved report body. | Not a market census; no verified inclusion frame, freshness/duplicate definition or comparable early-career definition. |

Aspen's monthly count can exceed an open-job snapshot without a contradiction. Different employer panels, occupation mixes and geographic assignment can further change counts. The observed roughly ninefold Headcount/Second Summit difference cannot be assigned to any one cause from this evidence. Do not convert Aspen's national daily/monthly relationship into a measured DC adjustment, apply Second Summit's entry-level fraction to all metro employment, or use capture–recapture: source independence and homogeneous inclusion probabilities are implausible and unmeasured.

[BLS's October 5 metro summary](https://www.bls.gov/regions/mid-atlantic/summary/blssummary_washington.pdf) reports August nonfarm employment of **3,299,600**, including **777,700** professional/business, **489,200** education/health and **663,300** government jobs. These employment stocks establish the importance of broad institutional sectors, not their current vacancy shares. They cannot be a vacancy denominator. [JOLTS openings](https://www.bls.gov/jlt/jltdef.htm) are positions open on a particular reference day with active external recruitment; they need not be online advertisements, and DC state totals are not the full MSA.

### Sensitivity and confidence

For transparency, the offline calculation shows what an incompatible division would produce: **216/165,437 = 0.131%; 216/50,772 = 0.425%; 216/5,687 = 3.798%**. These are **arithmetic illustrations only**, not three coverage estimates, not a plausible coverage interval and not statistical bounds. Numerator currentness, paid status, duplicate uncertainty and narrower geography are unresolved; denominators differ in date, window and observed population. Removing one explicitly expired listing would not solve any of those problems. Using the diagnostic 256-identity wider-geography set would likewise not fix denominator incompatibility.

**Best provisional result:** market-wide coverage A **not identifiable**; relevant early-career coverage B **not identifiable**; six-inventory source-local observation C **68 metadata items / 43 regional offered labels, zero matched to the saved engine capture**. Confidence is high in the frozen counts and individual captured requirements, moderate in stage diagnosis where public edges are incomplete, and low in any assertion about market-wide recall. There is no supported numeric market range to publish. Accessible missing opportunities are established; the total number missing is unknown.

## Where AI helps, and what remains unknown

This pilot uses AI for source research, ambiguous qualification interpretation, stage diagnosis and prioritization. Python remains authoritative for saved counts, hashes, identity arithmetic and reproduction. No model API/platform was added and no new metered services were activated. Future bounded AI assistance is useful for reading ambiguous requirements, proposing overlooked publisher types, distinguishing challenges from empty inventories, and adjudicating employer/duplicate or geography conflicts. Its runtime cost has not been benchmarked here. Hallucinated endpoints, mistaking preferred experience for a minimum, confident false merges and silent false-negative exclusions are the principal risks. AI hypotheses should create review tasks with evidence, not hard filters. Personalized ranking/fit can be evaluated later on a genuinely new independent sample; this research does not validate it.

Still unknown: the number of undiscovered sources; each provider's true employer universe; complete small-employer and local-government coverage; current paid vacancies hidden behind previews, sessions or restrictions; how many job ads represent multiple openings; how many postings are stale; the true remote-eligibility subset; and the broad early-career denominator. Congressional email lists, networking, member offices, referrals, recruiters and nonpublic channels create additional boundaries that an open-web engine cannot claim to cover.

To obtain a defensible *scoped* estimate next, pre-register a new dated frame independent of engine output—e.g. an external list of employers sampled within declared sector/size/geographic strata—and manually enumerate lawful paid regional inventories at the same date. Establish selection probabilities before extrapolating even to that frame. Log inaccessible/nonresponding employers separately; do not impute zero or pretend the frame is the whole market. This is more informative than refining an unsupported market percentage.
